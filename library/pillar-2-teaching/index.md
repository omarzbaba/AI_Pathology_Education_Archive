## Using AI to teach pathology to others

Pillar II is about the work that surrounds *delivering* teaching: the objectives, vignettes, slides, questions, narrative comments, and recommendation drafts that make a learning session land. The prompts here are written for the *faculty member or chief resident* who has 30 minutes between cases and needs to draft something teaching-shaped that actually works.

Principles for this pillar:

- **Treat AI output as a first draft, never the final draft.** A vignette without a faculty pass is a vignette without a faculty's judgment. The prompts here are explicit about what the human author owns and what the model can scaffold.
- **Specify level and context, every time.** "Write a transfusion reaction case" gives you something. "Write a transfusion reaction case for a PGY-2 in their first month of blood bank, where the pre-test probability of a specific reaction type is intentionally ambiguous" gives you something *teachable*.
- **The output is not a slide — it is an outline of a slide.** Even the best AI-drafted slide outline needs human staging: timing, beat structure, the moment of pause that makes the room think.
