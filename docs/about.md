## What this library is

The AI in Pathology Education Companion Library is the supporting material for a 50-minute workshop delivered at the **API Summit 2026** by Omar Z. Baba, MD: *AI in Pathology Education — A Practical Framework Across Learning, Teaching, and Educational Operations*.

The library has one job: take the framework introduced in the workshop and give you something concrete to use the next morning. Every prompt here is meant to be copied, adapted, and run against your own preferred AI model. Every worked example is meant to show what a thoughtful, edited use of one of these prompts actually looks like — not the polished marketing demo, but the real input-edit-final progression that a practicing pathologist would do.

## The three-pillar framework

The framework that organizes this library partitions AI use in pathology education into three pillars based on **who the AI is helping** and **what the artifact is**:

**Pillar I — Self-Education.** The AI is your tutor. You're learning. The artifact is your own mental model. Quality is measured by whether you retain the concept and can apply it.

**Pillar II — Teaching.** The AI is a draft assistant. You're teaching. The artifact is something you will deliver to learners. Quality is measured by whether the artifact lands with the audience.

**Pillar III — Educational Operations & Documentation.** The AI is an executive assistant. You're running an event or a program. The artifact is operational paperwork that surrounds the teaching. Quality is measured by whether the artifact does its job without distracting from the actual education.

These pillars are not mutually exclusive — a single 30-minute work session might touch all three. But thinking about them as separate domains helps because the **failure modes are different**: self-education fails through hallucinated facts you internalize, teaching fails through generic vignettes that don't engage, and operations fails through bland documents that the audience ignores.

## Who I am

I'm a clinical pathologist and pathology informaticist. I've been thinking and writing about practical AI use in pathology education for the past 18 months, mostly through small-group sessions with residents and a series of departmental in-services.

This library is the consolidated, organized version of patterns that emerged from those sessions. It is opinionated. It will not be opinionated in the same way at your institution, and that is fine — the prompts are designed to be adapted.

## What this library does NOT cover

This is important and intentional. The library does not address:

- **Diagnostic AI deployment.** Algorithms that contribute to patient care decisions live in a different regulatory and operational world. Educational use of AI ≠ clinical use of AI, and conflating them dilutes both conversations.
- **Hospital- or department-level AI governance.** Procurement, vendor evaluation, security review, and institutional policy are out of scope. If you need those, work with your CMIO and information security teams.
- **Scholarly publication assistance.** Drafting manuscripts with AI is its own topic with its own ethics and authorship considerations (see [Guardrails](library.html#/docs/guardrails)). The library covers the *educational* use of AI, not the publication pipeline.
- **Replacing human judgment.** Every prompt here is built around the assumption that a human pathologist makes the final call. The AI scaffolds; it does not author.
