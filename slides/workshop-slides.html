Practical foundations

What a good prompt looks like

Five components — skip any one and the model fills the gap with guesses

Most disappointing AI answers come from prompts missing one of these five. Add them all and you usually get a usable answer in one or two tries.

1

Who is the model

Role

Set the model's voice and frame of reference. Not decoration — it changes the texture of the output.

"Act as an experienced hematopathology attending teaching a PGY-2…"
2

What it can't infer

Context

Give what changes the answer — institution, learner level, specific scenario. Don't bloat with what's already known.

"PGY-2 in their first month of blood bank, two days of didactics…"
3

What you want

Task

A verb plus a clear deliverable. Number multi-part tasks. "Explain MGUS" is a topic; "explain in three layers…" is a task.

"Explain in three layers: 1-sentence, mechanistic, nuanced."
4

Shape of the answer

Format

Length, structure, named sections. Highest-leverage addition to a weak prompt — vague topic + specific format often works.

"Three paragraphs, layer name at the start of each, 3-5 sentences."
5

How it signals uncertainty

Verification cue

Most-skipped, most-regretted component. Without it, models produce fluent confidence whether they know or not.

"Flag any numerical threshold I should verify against current IMWG criteria."
Iterate in three rounds, not one. Draft — observe — refine. The diagnosis step (round 2 to round 3) is where the learning happens. The third version is usually the one worth saving.
